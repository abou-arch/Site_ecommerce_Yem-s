Understanding the Platforms
Key Differences
Every platform has unique characteristics that impact how we write copy. For
example, the approach for email marketing differs significantly from social media
ads. The user experience, attention span, and engagement levels vary across
platforms.
Email
●

Pros: Longer form, less distraction, higher engagement if opened.

●

Cons: Requires strong subject lines to ensure emails are opened.

Social Media (e.g., Facebook, Instagram)
●

Pros: High visibility, diverse audience targeting options.

●

Cons: High distraction, shorter attention span.

Video Platforms (e.g., YouTube)
●

Pros: Engaging format, visual and auditory elements.

●

Cons: Requires strong hooks within the first few seconds to retain viewers.

Adapting Your Copy
Core Principles
Regardless of the platform, the fundamental principles remain the same:
1.

Identify the Problem: Start by addressing the pain point your audience faces.

2. Validate the Internal Problem: Make them feel understood by
acknowledging their internal struggles.
3. Introduce the Philosophical Problem: Highlight the broader issue or belief
that exacerbates their pain point.
4. Present the Solution: Offer your product or service as the solution to their
problems.
5. Call to Action: Clearly state the next step you want them to take.

Platform-Specific Adjustments
Emails
●

Structure: Use longer, more detailed copy.

●

Flow: Take your time to build a narrative.

●

Engagement: Focus on crafting compelling subject lines to ensure high open
rates.

Social Media Ads
●

Structure: Use concise, attention-grabbing headlines.

●

Flow: Get to the point quickly. The first few seconds or lines must capture
attention.

●

Engagement: Use visuals and direct, engaging language to maintain interest.

Advertising Strategy
Objectives
Before writing any ad, clarify your objective. This influences your approach
significantly.
1.

Product Promotion: Selling a specific product directly.

2. Event Promotion: Encouraging attendance at a webinar, seminar, or event.
3. Lead Generation: Capturing contact information for future marketing efforts.
4. Content Promotion: Driving traffic to a piece of content, such as a blog post or
video.

Audience
Tailor your ads based on the audience's familiarity with your brand.
1.

Cold Audience: People who have never heard of you. Use comprehensive
structures to build rapport and trust.

2. Warm Audience: People who are somewhat familiar with your brand. Focus
on re-engagement and reminders.
3. Hot Audience: People who have shown strong interest or have previously
engaged. Use urgency and social proof to push them towards a purchase.

Structure 1: Problem-Why-Agitate-Solution (PWAS)
The Problem-Why-Agitate-Solution structure is straightforward and effective. Here’s
how it works:
1.

Problem: Start by identifying and stating the problem.

2. Why: Explain why the problem exists and what it causes.
3. Agitate: Intensify the problem, making the audience feel its pain more
acutely.
4. Solution: Present the solution, which could be your product or a call to action
like watching a video.
Example: Email Copy Using PWAS
Problem: Are you counting calories and following restrictive diets but still can't lose
weight?
Why: This might be because popular diets like low-carb and keto actually disrupt
your hunger hormones, causing your metabolism to slow down.
Agitate: The worst part is, if you're experiencing this hormone imbalance, it doesn't
matter how hard you try to lose weight, you're likely to fail.
Solution: To lose weight effectively, you first need to fix the hormone imbalance. I’ve
recorded a video explaining how to determine if you suffer from this problem and
what to do about it. Watch it here and never worry about counting calories again.

1

Pen to Profit | Plug & Play
Structure 2: Before-After-Bridge (BAB)
The Before-After-Bridge structure focuses on illustrating the problem (Before),
showcasing the desired outcome (After), and explaining how to achieve it (Bridge).
1.

Before: Describe the problem and its impact.

2. After: Paint a picture of the ideal situation once the problem is solved.
3. Bridge: Provide the solution that helps transition from the problem to the
ideal situation.
Example: Email Copy Using BAB
Before: Are you tired of looking at photos and feeling self-conscious because of your
weight? Do you compare yourself to others and worry about what they might be
thinking about you?
After: Imagine feeling confident in every photo, standing out for the right reasons,
and no longer worrying about what others think. Picture yourself enjoying social
events without self-doubt.
Bridge: Achieving this reality is simpler than you might think. Our holistic,
sustainable weight loss program focuses on nourishing your body rather than
depriving it. Learn how to regain control over your food and your life by watching this
video.

The Art of the Deal
Job Overview:
An appointment setter's primary responsibility is not to close deals but to book
appointments. This role is crucial as it bridges the gap between marketing efforts
and sales conversions. Appointment setters handle incoming leads generated from
various sources such as personal brands, ads, organic content, and more. These leads
need to be funneled into scheduled calls with sales representatives, making the role
of an appointment setter vital for an organization's success.
Types of Appointment Setters:
Phone Setters:
Responsibilities: Work from a CRM (Customer Relationship Management)
system to call potential leads, qualify them, and schedule appointments.
Tools Needed: Phone, CRM software.
Skills Required: Excellent phone etiquette, ability to follow a script, and strong
qualifying skills.
DM Setters:
Responsibilities: Manage and respond to direct messages on social media
platforms on behalf of influencers or brands.
Tools Needed: Social media accounts, messaging software (provided by the
company).
Skills Required: Strong written communication skills, ability to mimic the
influencer’s tone and style, and quick response time.

The Art of the Deal
The lead handling process is a critical component of the appointment setter's role,
ensuring that every potential customer is effectively guided from initial contact to a
scheduled appointment. This process can be broken down into several key stages:
1. Lead Generation
Lead generation is the initial step where potential customers (leads) are identified.
This can be done through various methods, including:
●

Paid Advertising: Ads on platforms like Facebook, Google, Instagram, and
LinkedIn attract potential customers by highlighting products or services.

●

Organic Content: Content such as blog posts, social media updates, YouTube
videos, and webinars that engage and attract an audience.

●

Video Sales Letters (VSLs): Videos that present an offer and encourage
viewers to take action, such as signing up for a webinar or providing their
contact information.

●

Lead Magnets: Free resources (e.g., eBooks, checklists, templates) offered in
exchange for contact information.

●

Webinars: Live or recorded presentations that provide valuable information
and capture the interest of potential customers.

2. Initial Contact
Once leads are generated, the appointment setter’s task is to make the initial
contact. This contact can be made via phone or direct message (DM):
●

Phone Contact: Using a CRM system, the appointment setter calls the lead to
introduce themselves and the company, and to start the conversation about
the potential fit for the product or service.

The Art of the Deal
●

DM Contact: For leads generated through social media, the appointment
setter sends a direct message from the company or influencer’s account to
start the conversation.

3. Lead Qualification
During the initial contact, the appointment setter’s primary goal is to qualify the
lead. This involves assessing whether the lead is a good fit for the product or service
being offered. Key aspects of qualification include:
●

Interest Level: Gauging the lead’s genuine interest in the product or service.

●

Financial Capability: Determining whether the lead has the financial means
to purchase the product or service.

●

Readiness to Buy: Understanding the lead’s urgency and timeline for making
a decision.

Qualification Questions:
●

What challenges are you currently facing?

●

How long have you been looking for a solution?

●

What budget have you allocated for this?

●

What specific features are you looking for in a solution?

●

When do you plan to make a decision?

4. Handling Objections
This is crucial for moving the conversation forward and securing the appointment.
Common objections might include:
●

Price Concerns: "I’m not sure I can afford this."

●

Timing Issues: "I need more time to think about it."

●

Need for More Information: "I need to know more about how this works."

Response Strategies:
●

Empathy: Acknowledge the lead’s concerns and show understanding.

●

Clarification: Provide clear and concise information to address the objection.

●

Value Proposition: Emphasize the unique value and benefits of the product
or service.

5. Booking the Appointment
Once the lead is qualified, the next step is to book the appointment with a sales
representative or closer. Key actions during this stage include:
●

Confirming Availability: Finding a suitable time for both the lead and the
sales representative.

●

Scheduling the Appointment: Using the company’s scheduling tools to book
the meeting.

●

Sending Confirmation: Providing the lead with a confirmation email or
message that includes the date, time, and details of the appointment.

The Art of the Deal
6. Follow-Up
Not all leads will be ready to book an appointment immediately. The appointment
setter should have a follow-up strategy to nurture these leads:
●

Follow-Up Calls or Messages: Regularly checking in with leads who need
more time.

●

Providing Additional Information: Sending resources, case studies, or
testimonials to address any remaining concerns.

●

Re-qualifying Leads: Periodically reassessing leads to determine if they are
ready to book an appointment.

7. Handoff to Sales Team
The final stage involves handing off the qualified lead to the sales team. Key points to
ensure a smooth transition include:
●

Detailed Notes: Providing the sales representative with detailed notes about
the lead’s needs, concerns, and qualification status.

●

Communication: Ensuring the sales representative understands the context
and background of the lead to personalize the follow-up conversation.

●

Preparation: Equipping the sales team with any additional information or
resources needed to close the deal.

Importance of Each Stage
Each stage in the lead handling process is crucial for maximizing the conversion rate
from lead to appointment and ultimately to a closed deal. By effectively managing
this process, appointment setters play a key role in the company’s sales success.

Marketing and Sales Integration: Marketing generates the leads, and sales close the

Conclusion: The role of an appointment setter is integral to the success of any
company that relies on scheduled calls to convert leads into sales. By mastering the
skills of qualification, communication, and professionalism, appointment setters can
significantly impact the company’s revenue and success.

Approach to Copywriting
Think of yourself not just as a writer but as a prosecutor presenting a case to a jury.
Your goal is to create a compelling argument that leads your audience to one
inevitable conclusion: buying your product. Just as a prosecutor must lead the jury to
believe the defendant is guilty, you must guide your audience to believe in the
necessity of your product.

Creating the Marketing Thesis
The first step in this process is to establish the marketing thesis. This is the central
idea that your entire copy will revolve around. It's what your audience needs to
believe to see the value in your product. The concept of the "one belief," coined by
Russell Brunson, is pivotal here.

The One Belief
The one belief states that a new opportunity is the key to the audience's desire and it
can only be achieved through your unique mechanism. This belief forms the
foundation of your marketing thesis.

Example Structure
"The [new opportunity] is the key to [desired result] and it is only achievable through
[unique mechanism]."

1

Pen to Profit | Plug & Play
Examples in Practice
1.

P90X: The new opportunity is avoiding the plateau effect. The desired result is
building muscle. The unique mechanism is the muscle confusion system.

2. Subway: The new opportunity is eating healthy fast food. The desired result is
losing weight. The unique mechanism is a diet based on Subway sandwiches.

Applying the One Belief
Step-by-Step Process
1.

Identify the Problem and Desire: Start with the external and internal
problems your audience faces, and their ultimate desire.

2. Establish the Duality and Common Enemy: Create a narrative that presents
the old, ineffective way (the common enemy) versus your new, effective way.
3. Craft the Marketing Thesis: Use the one belief structure to formulate your
core argument.

Example: Agency Navigation
●

Problem and Desire: The desire is to create a $10,000/month online business.
The external problem is making little money in a disliked job or unfulfilling
studies. The internal problem is feeling like time is wasted on these activities.

●

Marketing Thesis: "Starting an online business with the most
beginner-friendly business model is the key to making $10,000 a month in
record time and it's only achievable through selling high-ticket services."

1.

Introduce the Duality: Present the old, conventional path as ineffective and

2. Create Meaning for New Concepts: Throughout your promotion, define and

3. Lead to the One Belief: Guide your audience to see your product as the

Structuring Your Copy
The Launch Canvas
The Launch Canvas is a template that helps you organize your thoughts and
structure your copy. It includes:
1.

Problem and Desire: Identify and articulate the core problems and desires of
your audience.

2. Marketing Thesis: Formulate your one belief that serves as the foundation of
your argument.
3. Top of the Mountain and Rock Bottom: Define the best possible outcome
and the worst-case scenario for your audience.
4. Common Enemy: Identify and articulate the common enemy that your
audience must overcome.

Example: Weight Loss Scenario
●

Problem and Desire: Desire to lose weight and feel confident. External
problem is being overweight. Internal problem is feeling unattractive.

●

Marketing Thesis: "The key to sustainable weight loss is managing metabolic

●

Top of the Mountain: Achieving a fit and healthy body.

●

Rock Bottom: Remaining overweight and unhappy.

●

Common Enemy: Traditional diets that don't address metabolic balance.

Avoiding Objections
In this module, Paul discusses the different ways to prevent objections, so that you
can achieve sales success with confidence.

Agenda
In this module, we will cover 2 main topics:
1 . Understanding objections
2. Perceiving objections

Understanding Objections
Objections are merely symptoms of poor discovery and transition.
Many sales reps focus way too much on objection-handling.
“Good sales reps focus on objection-handling; great sales reps focus on
objection-prevention.”

The Million Dollar Process

Perceiving Objections
There are 3 stages in the evolution of any sales rep:
1. Focusing on objections
2. Ignoring objections
3. Pretending objections don’t exist
At the beginning, you get objections because you are simply not good enough at
sales
You are not good at building that ‘gap’ or agitating the pain
You don’t understand how to build opportunity cost or tackle doubt and so on…
As a result, objections and ‘no’s’ become synonymous
Which makes you think that, in order to turn a ‘no’ into a ‘yes’, you must tackle the
objections themselves
In reality, a much better perspective to have is how you can prevent an objection
from happening in the first place
And the answer to that is by doing properdiscoveryandtransition
When you focus onmastering your discovery and transition,you will find that
closing deals becomeseamless
“Not every close has to have an objection.”

The Million Dollar Process

Shifting Perspectives
When you obsess over something, you will probably get more of that one thing
“Sales reps who obsess over objections are likely to get more objections at the
end of their sales calls.”
Don’t focus too much on the objections or predicting which ones you will get
Sales reps who focus too much on objections end up ‘creating’ or manifesting
objections in their calls

Dismantling Objections
Handling objections is not ‘sexy’
But, you must still know how to handle them
They are an important part of the sales call and may well take place
Just because you should not focus too much on them does not mean you should
ignore them
Your prospect might be scared to take that first step and they might need your
help deciding
Focus on dismantling objections early in the call so that they do not arise later
on
Overall, don’t over-complicate it and focus on what really matters which is
understanding your prospect and their concerns

The Million Dollar Process

Summary
Prevent objections through proper discovery and transition, rather than focusing
solely on handling them. Understand that objections are symptoms of poor sales
techniques and shift your perspective to avoid creating objections during sales calls.

Weekly Emails
The weekly emails are the easy part. These emails are sent on an ongoing basis,
typically from one to three times a week. They serve as the backbone of your email
marketing strategy, providing consistent touchpoints with your audience.
Types of Weekly Emails
1.

Content Emails: These emails provide valuable content that leads to insights.
The goal is to create those "aha" moments where your audience connects the
dots and gains a deeper understanding of a topic.

2. Hook Emails: These emails serve as triggers for your sales campaigns. They
invite your audience to participate in events, watch videos, or take other
actions that lead them into your sales funnel.
Creating Insightful Content
To create content that leads to insights, use the philosophical problem approach.

Introduction
Understanding and structuring your offer correctly is crucial because it ensures a
smooth transition from your content to your pitch, making the sales process feel
natural and compelling rather than abrupt and jarring.

The Importance of Understanding Your Offer
Most copywriters make the mistake of treating the content and the pitch as two
separate parts of their promotion. They spend most of their time on content and
then abruptly switch to the pitch, which can catch the audience off guard and lead
to resistance. To avoid this, you need to integrate the offer into your content from the
very beginning, creating a seamless transition that makes the sale feel like the next
logical step.

Structuring Your Offer
Approach
Unlike traditional methods that focus on features and benefits, I approach offers in
terms of "pillars" – key elements that fill gaps created throughout the promotion.
Each pillar represents a critical aspect of the solution that addresses the problems
and desires identified in your audience.

Pen to Profit | Plug & Play

accountability.
3. Accountability: Expert coaches and peers within the community ensure that
the audience stays on track, providing guidance and support.
4. Self-Improvement: Beyond business success, the audience needs to be
mentally and physically at their best. This includes personal development
resources to enhance all aspects of their lives.

and integrates them seamlessly into your offer.

The Pyramid of Beliefs
Remember the pyramid from Module 2, where we discussed experiences,
observations, assumptions, conclusions, and beliefs? This pyramid represents how
people form their understanding of reality. At the top of the pyramid is a self-sealing
logic bubble, where people only accept messages that align with their beliefs and
experiences.

Key Steps in the Copywriting Formula
This formula consists of six simple steps designed to get your message through the
self-sealing logic bubble and drive action:
1.

Verify Their Experience

2. Validate the Internal Problem
3. Pivot to the Philosophical Problem
4. Provide an Explanation
5. Offer a Benefit
6. Call to Action

Step-by-Step Breakdown
1. Verify Their Experience
Start by stating something your audience has experienced or seen before. This aligns
your message with their beliefs and grabs their attention.
Example: Does the thought of taking photos fill you with dread, almost as if before
you even take a picture, you already know you won't like the person you see?

1

Pen to Profit | Plug & Play
2. Validate the Internal Problem
Use the internal problem to validate their feelings. This builds an emotional
connection and shows that you understand their frustrations and fears.
Example: This isn't just about photos; it permeates your thoughts, affecting your
ability to enjoy the moment. You start avoiding photos, a constant reminder of the
gap between where you are and where you want to be.
3. Pivot to the Philosophical Problem
Use the internal problem to introduce the philosophical problem, creating a conflict.
This is where you highlight the deeper issue and add a sense of duality.
Example: Most people I've talked to have tried to fix this before and failed. They’ve
tried low-carb diets, keto, and restrictive diets. That's exactly the problem—restrictive
diets make you feel even more hungry.
4. Provide an Explanation
Explain why the problem exists. This logical argument justifies their emotional
reaction and builds credibility.
Example: Here's why: Restrictive diets slow down your metabolism and turn on your
hunger hormones, making weight loss even harder.
5. Offer a Benefit
Add a benefit to show them the positive outcome of solving the problem.
Example: When you understand how your hunger hormones work, you can lose

Understanding the Elephant Analogy
To illustrate the concept of perception and reality, let’s use the Elephant Analogy.
Imagine several blind people are asked to touch different parts of an elephant and
describe what they feel. One touches the trunk and says it’s a snake, another touches
the leg and says it’s a tree trunk, and another touches the ear and says it’s a fan. Each
person’s reality is based on their limited perspective, yet none of them are entirely
wrong. This analogy highlights that everyone perceives reality differently based on
their unique experiences and beliefs.
The Pyramid of Beliefs
Every individual has a "pyramid" that shapes their view of the world. This pyramid is
built from their experiences, observations, assumptions, and beliefs. Here’s how it
works:
1.

Experiences and Observations: These form the base of the pyramid and
include everything a person has gone through in life.

2. Assumptions: Based on their experiences, people make assumptions about
how the world works.
3. Conclusions: From these assumptions, they draw conclusions.
4. Beliefs: These conclusions solidify into beliefs, which guide their decisions and
worldview.
We all sit atop our pyramids, believing our view of reality is accurate. However, it’s just
a perspective shaped by our unique experiences.

1

Pen to Profit | Plug & Play
Self-Sealing Logic
Within our pyramids, we have what’s called a "self-sealing logic." This means we only
accept information that fits within our existing beliefs and reject anything that
doesn’t. This logic creates a bubble of what we consider to be true, and anything
outside of it is often dismissed as false.
Steps to Influencing Beliefs
To get your customer to accept what you say, you must first understand their current
beliefs and then carefully guide them to new conclusions. Here’s a step-by-step
process:
1.

Understand Their Current Beliefs: Research your audience to understand
their experiences, assumptions, and beliefs. This forms the basis of their reality.

2. Meet Them Where They Are: Start your communication by acknowledging
and validating their current beliefs. This builds trust and rapport.
3. Introduce New Ideas Gradually: Use logical and emotional arguments to
introduce new ideas slowly. Plant seeds of doubt about their current beliefs
and provide evidence that supports your new ideas.
4. Guide Them to Conclusions: Lead them to new conclusions by presenting
compelling reasons and benefits of your perspective. Make them feel that
reaching these conclusions is their own idea.

2

Pen to Profit | Plug & Play
Communicate Emotionally and Logically
People make decisions emotionally and then justify them with logic. Therefore, your
copy should first connect emotionally and then provide logical reasons to support
the decision.
●

Emotional Connection: Use stories, vivid descriptions, and empathy to create
an emotional bond with your audience.

●

Logical Justification: Follow up with facts, statistics, testimonials, and other
logical arguments to back up the emotional decision.

Effective Communication Patterns
To break through the self-sealing logic and influence your audience's beliefs, use
effective communication patterns. Here’s a basic framework:
1.

Acknowledge Current Beliefs: Start by validating their current beliefs to show

2. Introduce New Information: Present new information that challenges their

3. Provide Evidence: Use logical arguments, testimonials, and data to support

4. Lead to New Beliefs: Guide them to adopt new beliefs by showing the

Conclusion
Influencing your customer’s decisions requires understanding their unique

Every piece of copy you write can evoke two fundamental emotions: moving towards
pleasure or moving away from fear. Depending on your copy's objective, you can use
either or both emotions interchangeably to craft your message.
Moving Towards Pleasure
This emotion appeals to the desire for positive outcomes. Examples include:
●

Achieve your goals.

●

Lose weight and feel great.

●

Gain confidence and respect.

Moving Away From Fear
This emotion appeals to the need to avoid negative outcomes. Examples include:
●

Stop feeling anxious in social situations.

●

Avoid gaining more weight.

●

Prevent the embarrassment of being ignored at work.

The Power of Fear Over Pleasure
People are generally more motivated to avoid pain than to seek pleasure. This is
rooted in our survival instincts, where avoiding danger takes precedence over
seeking enjoyment. For example:
●

People are more likely to take action to avoid losing $100 than to gain $100.

Fear of a negative outcome often prompts immediate action.

Levels of Awareness
The concept of levels of awareness was first introduced in Eugene Schwartz's book,
"Breakthrough Advertising." There are five different levels of awareness that your
prospects can have about your product and industry:
1.

Completely Unaware: These prospects are not aware of their problem or the
solution.

2. Problem Aware: These prospects are aware that they have a problem but do
not know that solutions exist.
3. Solution Aware: These prospects know about their problem and that
solutions exist, but they don't know about your specific solution.
4. Product Aware: These prospects know about the different solutions available,
including your product.
5. Most Aware: These prospects know your product, your competitors, and the
industry well.
Depending on the level of awareness, you should lead with a different message to
match their specific level.

1

Pen to Profit | Plug & Play

Eight Types of Leads
Here are the eight types of leads that you can use to start your promotion:
1.

Offer Lead: Lead with an offer, mentioning the product, price, and guarantees
early in the message. This is effective for the most aware prospects who know
your product and need a nudge to convert.
○

Example: "Get Dan Kennedy's book for just $1!"

2. Invitation Lead: Similar to the offer lead but more subtle and exclusive. This is
suitable for products with a higher price point where more convincing is
needed.
○

Example: "Would you like me to double, triple, or even quadruple your
business for free?"

3. Promise Lead: Lead with a promise or a claim about the result people will
experience with your product. This is ideal for problem-aware and
solution-aware prospects.
○

Example: "Reduce your wrinkles by up to 56% in the first month!"

4. Problem-Solution Lead: Start by identifying a problem and then present the
solution. This is effective for problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware,
and most aware prospects.
○

Example: "Never again do your hands and feet feel like blocks of ice."

5. Secret Lead: Use the allure of a secret to draw in the audience. This works well
in highly competitive industries where you need to differentiate your solution.
○

Example: "The best business to start in 2023."

6. Proclamation Lead: Make a bold prediction or statement to capture attention.
This is useful for less aware prospects to create awareness of the problem.
○

Example: "Why safe investors may lose everything within the next six
months."

2

Pen to Profit | Plug & Play
7. Story Lead: Start with a compelling story that draws the audience in and
connects to the problem and solution. This is effective for skeptical audiences.
○

Example: "I had one foot in the grave and a banana peel under the
other. My doctor sent me to the nursing home to die."

8. Direct Lead: A straightforward approach that gets straight to the point, often
used for the most aware prospects.
○

Example: "Our new product is here – get it now!"

Lead Checklist
To ensure your lead is effective, it must accomplish four specific principles:
1.

Expand on the Big Idea: Your lead should elaborate on the central theme of
your promotion.

2. Sell the Viewership: Make the audience want to continue watching or
reading by setting high stakes or promises.
3. Set the Stakes: Explain why the audience should care and what they stand to
gain or lose.
4. Provide Proof of Promise: Back up your claims with evidence, though this
may not always be necessary depending on the context.

Conclusion
Understanding and using the appropriate type of lead based on your audience's level
of awareness is crucial to the success of your promotion. By tailoring your lead to
match where your prospect is in their awareness journey, you can create a
compelling and engaging message that draws them in and sets the stage for a
successful promotion.

Setup 2
● Have a monitor with a camera that is slightly above eye-level

Lighting
You don’t need to overcomplicate this.
Make sure your lighting is appropriate.
Two key bits of lighting to pay attention to:
1. Foreground lighting
2. Background lighting
Additionally:

● You could sit in front of a window but be careful that if it is too bright, you will

night

Setting
The best and easiest background to use as a beginner is a plain white background.
You cannot go wrong with it!
4 things to avoid
1. Clutter or messiness
2. Beds
3. Movement
4. Windows
Overall, avoid anything that will make you look unprofessional.

Clothing
You don’t have to wear a suit for every call.
In fact, a suit might even make your prospect feel uncomfortable.
Whatever you do, make sure:
● Your clothes are not wrinkled
● Dress to impress, as if you were going on a date
● Don’t wear ties
